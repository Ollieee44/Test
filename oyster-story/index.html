01 · The size problem

Protein degraders, made small enough to reach the brain.

Degraders remove disease-causing proteins instead of blocking them. Most are too large to be taken by mouth or to enter the brain.

02 · Split

One degrader, two small halves.

A reversible linker lets a SELFTAC® molecule travel as two small molecules.

03 · The barrier

Through the cells, not between them.

Tight junctions seal the brain’s capillaries. Each half is small enough to pass through the endothelial cells themselves.

04 · Into the neuron

Then across one more membrane, into the neuron.

Still small, each half slips through the cell membrane on its own, into the neuron that makes the target protein.

05 · Rebuilt

Inside the neuron, the halves click back together.

One end holds the target protein, the other an E3 ligase.

06 · Cleared

Tagged, then destroyed.

An E2 enzyme hands ubiquitin onto the target, and the proteasome breaks it down.

Then it does it again.

This is the 3D story from the Science page of Website v2, on its own. It runs on three.js (loaded from a CDN) and plays as its section scrolls past. The first four files below are all it needs; the tool rebuilds the shapes. Download them together as a zip, or use Copy and View to take one file at a time.

All the files below, in one folder.

The files

  • oyster-story.js: the scene, the camera path and the scroll timeline (about 500 lines)
  • oyster-story.css: the pinned stage, copy, rail, end card and the six palettes
  • meshes.json: the protein and molecule shapes, traced from PDB 5T35 (about 770 KB)
  • story-markup.html: the story's HTML, to paste where it should appear
  • tools/build_meshes.py: rebuilds meshes.json from protein structures (Python)
  • tools/meshes.config.json: what the shapes are built from, and how
  • README.md: the full guide, including everything below

Change the 3D shapes

The proteins and the degrader come from crystal structure 5T35 (BRD4, MZ1 and VHL with Elongin B and C), and ubiquitin from 1UBQ. tools/build_meshes.py rebuilds meshes.json from them, following tools/meshes.config.json:

pip install numpy scipy scikit-image rdkit
python3 tools/build_meshes.py --out meshes-new.json
  • Proteins: a soft surface about 3 Å outside each protein's atoms, from a Gaussian blob per atom, then smoothed.
  • The two halves: MZ1 cut at its PEG linker; each half drawn flat, textbook style, as a tube along every bond, facing the viewer where it binds.
  • Try before you swap: put data-meshes="meshes-new.json" on the story's <section> to load the new file, and rename it to meshes.json once you like it.
  • Another structure or degrader: change the PDB id, the chains, the ligand code and its linker atoms in the config. The guide in README.md covers each setting.

The shipped meshes.json is the website's original. The tool matches its proteins to within about 0.6 Å; the halves come out in the same style but not identical.

Change the barrier, neuron and proteasome

These are drawn by the script, from the SCENE block near the top of oyster-story.js: the number and size of the barrier's cells and how far down it sits, the neuron's membrane height, radius and bilayer gap, and the proteasome's rings and caps. The halves' path down through the membranes follows these numbers, so they still pause inside each one. Colours are in the PAL table above it.

Good to know

  • Palette: set data-palette on <html> to nacre, tidepool, pearlink, shellcoral, abyssmint or charcoalviolet. Any .pal button[data-pal] on the page switches it live.
  • Length: the story is 1650vh tall (1300vh on phones). Change .story { height } in the CSS to make it faster or slower.
  • Words: edit the seven .beat blocks in the markup. Their timing is the BEATS list in the script.
  • Colours of the 3D parts: the PAL table near the top of oyster-story.js.
  • Inside a Claude artifact: another artifact can copy these files straight from this one, so nothing needs downloading.
  • The story respects reduced-motion settings, and says so if the browser has no WebGL.
