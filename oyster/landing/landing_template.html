01 · The size problem

Protein degraders, made small enough to reach the brain.

Degraders remove disease-causing proteins instead of blocking them. Most are too large to be taken by mouth or to enter the brain.

02 · Split

One degrader, two small halves.

A reversible linker lets a SELFTAC® molecule travel as two small molecules.

03 · The barrier

Through the cells, not between them.

Tight junctions seal the brain’s capillaries. Each half is small enough to pass through the endothelial cells themselves.

04 · Into the neuron

Then across one more membrane, into the neuron.

Still small, each half slips through the cell membrane on its own, into the neuron that makes the target protein.

05 · Rebuilt

Inside the neuron, the halves click back together.

One end holds the target protein, the other an E3 ligase.

06 · Cleared

Tagged, then destroyed.

An E2 enzyme hands ubiquitin onto the target, and the proteasome breaks it down.

07 · Again

Then it does it again.

The degrader is not used up. It lets go and catches the next one.

Oral, brain-penetrant protein degraders.
