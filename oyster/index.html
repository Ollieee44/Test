Vision

We are building a pipeline of medicines on one idea: turn large protein degraders into small molecules, so targeted protein degradation can reach patients as oral drugs, including in the brain.

Our approach
Approach

Two small molecules. One degrader, assembled where it is needed.

The size problem

Bifunctional degraders are large molecules. Most cannot be absorbed from the gut or pass into the brain.

A reversible linker

SELFTAC® places a reversible linker between the two halves, turning one large degrader into two small molecules.

Through the blood-brain barrier

Tight junctions seal the gaps between the endothelial cells of brain capillaries, so a drug has to pass through the cells themselves. Small molecules can.

Self-assembly in the neuron

Inside the cell the two halves recombine into the full degrader. One end holds the target protein, the other an E3 ligase.

Tagged and cleared

The E3 ligase tags the target with ubiquitin and the proteasome breaks it down. The degrader is released to act again.

Why Oyster

Something small, built into something remarkable.

A pearl forms inside the shell. SELFTAC® molecules work in a similar way: they enter as small drugs and assemble into a degrader inside the cell.
