Degraders that reach the brain

Oral, CNS-penetrant protein degraders

Making protein degraders small

Our goal is to bring the power of targeted protein degradation to medicines that patients can take as a pill, including for diseases of the brain.

Approach

Two small molecules. One degrader, assembled where it is needed.

Bifunctional degraders are versatile and can be designed rationally, but their large size gives them poor drug-like properties.

SELFTAC® places a reversible linker between the two halves of a degrader. The result is two small molecules that can be absorbed orally and cross the blood-brain barrier, then self-assemble into the full degrader inside the cell.

Conventional degraderToo large to cross the endothelium
01 / The size problem

Too large to travel

Bifunctional degraders are large molecules. Most cannot be absorbed from the gut or pass into the brain.

02 / Reversible linker

Split in two

SELFTAC® places a reversible linker between the two halves, turning one large degrader into two small molecules.

03 / Blood-brain barrier

Through the barrier

Tight junctions seal the gaps between the endothelial cells of brain capillaries, so a drug has to pass through the cells themselves. Small molecules can.

04 / Self-assembly

Rebuilt in the neuron

Inside the cell the two halves recombine into the full degrader. One end holds the target protein, the other an E3 ligase.

05 / Degradation

Tagged and cleared

The E3 ligase tags the target with ubiquitin and the proteasome breaks it down. The degrader is released to act again.
