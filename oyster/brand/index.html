The Open Shell

Two valves hold a pearl. It is the company name drawn as simply as possible, and it describes the science: two small molecules that come together to form something valuable inside the cell.

The idea

01

Two valves

An oyster is two shells joined at a hinge. SELFTAC® is two small molecules. The lower valve is cupped and the upper one is flatter, as in a real oyster.

02

An open hinge

The valves do not quite touch at the hinge. The small gap stands for the reversible linker that lets the two halves separate and rejoin.

03

A pearl inside

A pearl forms inside the shell from something small. The degrader assembles inside the cell. The pearl carries the accent colour in every palette.

Colour versions

Use the full-colour mark wherever possible. The one-colour versions are for print, embossing and places where colour is not available. In those, the pearl is slightly smaller so it stays separate from the lower valve.

Using the mark

Clear space

Keep a margin of at least one pearl width on every side. Nothing else should enter that area.

Do not

Rotate, stretch or outline the mark, close the hinge gap, move the pearl, or recolour the pearl to match the shell in a full-colour setting. Do not place the full-colour mark on busy imagery. Use the reversed or one-colour version instead.

Directions explored

Three routes were drawn. The Open Shell was chosen because it names the company and explains the science in a single image.

Chosen

The Open Shell

An oyster, a pearl and a two-part story. It is still recognisable at 16px.

Alternative

Nacre rings

Growth lines forming an O. It is elegant, but at small sizes it looks like a target symbol.

Alternative

Pearl halves

A sphere made from two halves. The story is clear, but it sits close to a generic pill icon.
